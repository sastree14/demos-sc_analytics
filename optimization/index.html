REFERENCE NOTESASSUMPTIONS / SOLVER

Transparent by design

Every result is derived from a defined calculation, rather than an unexplained recommendation or a scripted number.

01 / INPUT DATA

Four fictional product lines

Each line has a demand estimate, on-hand inventory, per-unit cost, contribution margin, shortage penalty, holding cost and maximum order. These are illustrative assumptions, not measurements from a real business.

02 / OBJECTIVE

Expected business contribution

Maximise modelled contribution from fulfilled demand, less an explicit penalty for missed demand, expected stock-carry cost and a small financing charge on purchased units. Purchase cost is not counted again as a profit expense because the unit contribution already reflects sold-unit costs.

03 / CONSTRAINTS

Budget and capacity

Purchases cannot exceed the available investment or additional storage capacity, and each product has a maximum order. Orders move in multiples of ten units; no fractional replenishment is allowed.

04 / SOLVER

Exact within the stated increments

Multiple-choice dynamic programming explores feasible choices for every SKU, with budget tracked in €50 units and storage in 10-unit batches. It selects the highest objective value from the feasible states, not a heuristic guess.

05 / COMPARISON

A clear reference policy

The benchmark adds replenishment batches to the currently least-covered product until it runs out of feasible budget or capacity. This is a disclosed comparison rule—not a claim about how any real client currently operates.

06 / UNCERTAINTY

Illustrative demand distributions

Expected demand uses five symmetric, weighted scenarios. Demand volatility and missed-demand sensitivity are adjustable. The scenario cards test the same optimised plan under different market conditions; they are not probability-calibrated forecasts.
